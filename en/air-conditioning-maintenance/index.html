Why maintenance pays off

An air conditioner collects dust, moisture and residue in its filters, coils and drain. Over time that cuts performance, raises running costs, causes smells and can lead to water leaks. Regular servicing lets us spot wear before it becomes a breakdown, and keeps the air you breathe cleaner.

What a service includes

  • Cleaning or replacing filters and cleaning the indoor units.
  • Cleaning evaporator and condenser coils and the drain pan.
  • Checking the drain for blockages.
  • Checking the refrigerant circuit, including pressures and possible leaks — work reserved for F-Gas certified technicians.
  • Electrical checks: connections, current draw and protections.
  • Operating test in cooling and heating, and a check of remote and sensors.
  • A summary of the equipment's condition and recommendations where relevant.

How often?

As a rule of thumb, once a year for a home unit. Shops, heavily used equipment, dusty or greasy environments or locations close to the sea may call for more frequent visits. For larger systems in commercial buildings, regulations may require periodic maintenance and inspection, and we can help you understand what applies.

What you can do between services

  • Clean the indoor filters as the manual says.
  • Keep the outdoor unit clear of leaves, plastic and obstacles.
  • Do not cover units or stack things against them.
  • Act early on smells, new noises or drips.
  • Before summer and winter, run the unit and make sure it cools and heats normally.

Who it is for

  • Homeowners and tenants — one-off or annual servicing.
  • Businesses — planned maintenance contracts for offices, shops and restaurants.
  • Buildings with VRV/VRF — plans adapted to the system and its use.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I service my air conditioner?

For home use, once a year is a good reference. Heavy use, commercial premises or harsher environments may justify more often.

Can I clean the filters myself?

Yes. You can clean the indoor filters by following the manual. Coil cleaning and refrigerant-circuit checks should be done by a technician.

Does servicing include refrigerant top-up?

Checking for leaks and pressures is part of the service. A top-up is only done when justified, and if there is a leak it should be found and fixed first. Any work on the refrigerant circuit must be done by a certified technician.

Do you offer maintenance contracts for businesses?

Yes. We set up a visit plan suited to the number of units and how the space is used. Contact us to discuss your case.

Why does my air conditioner smell?

Usually moisture and residue in the filters, coil or drain. A professional clean normally solves it. If the smell persists, it needs diagnosing.
